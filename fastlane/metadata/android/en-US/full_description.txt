Brainwave catches a thought before it escapes.

Open the app, hit the big button, and say what is on your mind. Brainwave
transcribes it, writes a title for you, and looks for a deadline in what you
said. If it cannot find one, it asks you out loud — and listens for the answer,
through your headset if you are wearing one.

Every brainwave is then emailed to you with the original recording attached, and
the due date goes straight into your calendar, so the thought lands in the two
places you actually look: your inbox and your calendar. Your phone also reminds
you when the moment arrives.

On the home screen, swipe a brainwave left to complete it or right to delete it,
sort by due date or title in either direction, and tap one open to edit the text.
You can also email yourself the whole list in one message.

Brainwave has no accounts, no analytics and no Google Play dependencies.
Speech recognition runs on the phone itself, from a speech model the app downloads
once (about 40 MB), so your voice never leaves the phone. The only network calls are to your own SMTP server and — if you
switch it on — the Claude API for sharper titles and dates.
